bio_en: |
 For more than ten years, Brazilian jiu-jitsu has held a central place in my life. My passion began in 1994, the night I rented a VHS cassette of UFC 1 with a friend. Seeing a martial art based on technique rather than brute strength dominate the cage left a deep impression on me. I told myself that one day, I would learn that style. That moment sparked a curiosity that has never left me.

 I trained in other martial arts over the years and even earned a black belt, but I always had a lingering pull toward ground fighting. In 2012, I finally had the opportunity to start learning BJJ—at first for mixed martial arts—and later by enrolling in a school an hour from home. The moment I put on a gi for the first time, with my brand-new white belt, I instantly fell in love. Gradually, I began to step away from the other arts I had practiced and taught.

 In 2016, while I was a blue belt, I opened the first BJJ school in my region, in Cowansville—not only to give myself the chance to spend more time on the mats without driving long distances, but also to begin sharing my passion. In the years that followed, a friend—who naturally became my coach—joined the team, and we moved the academy to Bromont. A few months before COVID, I decided to move on and join a school in Granby, where I was able to continue teaching and sharing my passion.

 Over time, my curiosity evolved into a structured approach. Alongside my progression as an athlete, I built a career in continuous improvement, eventually earning my Lean Six Sigma Black Belt certification. In my professional life, I work as an analyst and method optimization specialist—an experience that has shaped the way I approach jiu-jitsu:

 - analyzing systems

 - optimizing sequences

 - simplifying complex principles

 - improving the learning process for my students

 Today, this analytical approach is at the heart of my teaching. It allows me to present jiu-jitsu in a clear, methodical, and accessible way, whether to beginners or high-level competitors.

 With more than two decades of coaching experience (basketball, alpine skiing, music, martial arts, and more), I’ve worked with people from all walks of life: athletes, teams, children, adults, hobbyists, passionate practitioners, and competitors. Having competed in multiple disciplines throughout my life, I’ve witnessed firsthand the mental, physical, and technical challenges every student faces. This has allowed me to develop a rare ability to adapt my teaching so that each person can progress at their own pace—without losing the rigor required for excellence.

 A major milestone in my journey is my involvement with BJJ Fanatics, the largest jiu-jitsu instructional platform in the world, where I’ve been teaching for more than four years. This platform gives me the opportunity to share my knowledge and methods with students from all around the globe.

 In 2025, my path took a new turn. Along with a close group of friends, we decided to open our own school. At the time, I was teaching at two places—one BJJ school and occasionally at Speedy Gym in Granby. Speedy generously allowed us to use their space while we searched for our own, and from there, we opened our new academy, now known as Armada Jiu-Jitsu.
